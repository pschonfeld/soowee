equipment:
  - Small saucepan, light inside, so you can see the butter brown
  - Fine-mesh sieve

directions:
  - title: Make the brown-butter milk
    steps:
      - Melt {35 g butter} in the saucepan over medium heat and let it foam for {timer:3-4m}, swirling, until the foam settles, the specks at the bottom turn light hazelnut brown and it smells nutty (beurre noisette). Stop before it goes darker.
      - Take the pan off the heat and carefully pour in {500 ml whole milk}; it will sputter.
      - Set it over low heat and stir, scraping up the brown specks, until the milk feels just warm to the finger, about 35–40 °C. This brown-butter milk is all the liquid for the batter; the butter floats on top, so stir it before each pour.
      - tip: Warm milk carries the butter into the batter as fine droplets. Poured straight into cold milk and eggs, melted butter sets into flecks that the sieve then catches.
  - title: Mix the batter
    steps:
      - Whisk {225 g plain flour}, {10 g sugar} and {0.5 tsp salt} in a large bowl and make a well in the centre.
      - Beat {3 eggs} in a jug until no streaks of white remain, and pour them into the well.
      - Whisk from the centre outwards, drawing the flour in gradually, and add about {150 ml} of the brown-butter milk as it stiffens. Keep whisking for {timer:1m} after the flour is in, until you have a thick, completely smooth paste, like thick yoghurt.
      - tip: Lumps only break up while the paste is thick enough to resist the whisk. In a thin batter they float away from it, so add the milk only once the paste is smooth.
      - Whisk in the rest of the brown-butter milk in three additions, and let each one go smooth before the next. Stir rather than beat, so you don’t whisk in air.
      - Pour the batter through the sieve into a jug or a container with a lid. Only a few specks should be left behind.
  - title: Rest the batter
    steps:
      - Cover and leave at room temperature for {rest:1h}, or refrigerate overnight, up to 24 hours. If it was in the fridge, take it out 30 minutes before cooking.
      - tip: Resting lets the flour absorb the liquid, so the crêpes are tender and don’t tear, and lets the air bubbles escape. The batter thickens a little as it rests.
  - title: Cook the crêpes
    heat: Heat the crêpe pan over **medium-high** heat for 3–4 minutes, until a drop of water dances and vanishes in 1–2 seconds.
    steps:
      - Whisk the batter well, since the butter and flour settle. After a night in the fridge a few specks of set butter are fine, as they melt in the pan, so don’t sieve it again.
      - It should be like pouring cream, coating a spoon thinly and running off in a quick ribbon. If it is thicker, whisk in up to {50 ml whole milk}, a tablespoon at a time.
      - Find a ladle or cup that holds about 50 ml, measured once with water, so every crêpe gets the same amount.
      - Wipe the pan with a paper towel dipped in {neutral oil}, leaving a sheen but no drops. The butter in the batter is usually enough after that; wipe again only if a crêpe sticks.
      - Lift the pan off the heat, pour one ladleful of batter into the centre and at once tilt and swirl the pan so the batter covers the base in one thin layer.
      - tip: The first crêpe is a test. If the batter sets before you can swirl it, the pan is too hot or the batter too thick; if the crêpe is still pale after a minute, turn the heat up.
      - Cook for {timer:45-60s}, until the surface looks dry, the edge lifts and turns lacy and the underside is golden in spots.
      - Loosen the edge with the spatula, flip and cook the second side for {timer:15-20s}, until it sets.
      - Stack the crêpes on a plate under a clean tea towel, where they stay warm and supple. Serve them with the first, golden side outwards.
      - tip: For another pan, scale the batter with the base area, about 35 ml for 20 cm and 60–70 ml for 28 cm.

## Chef’s Notes

- **Make ahead:** Make the batter the evening before and cook in the morning; it keeps up to 24 hours in the fridge. Whisk it well and adjust the thickness before cooking.
- **Store:** Cooked crêpes keep 2 days in the fridge, stacked and wrapped, or 2 months in the freezer with baking paper between them. Warm them for 10 seconds a side in a hot, dry pan.
- **Swap:** For savoury fillings, leave out the sugar. For the most neutral crêpe, melt the butter in the warm milk without browning it. Vanilla, zest or liqueur belong in the filling, not the batter.
- **Serve:** Butter and sugar, or sugar and a squeeze of lemon; jam or chestnut cream; or savoury, with ham, grated Gruyère and an egg.

## Allergens

- **Gluten:** flour.
- **Egg:** eggs.
- **Milk:** milk and butter.
