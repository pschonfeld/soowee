steps:
  - title: Make the coffee
    text:
      - Brew {400 ml} strong coffee or espresso and set it aside to cool completely, about {wait:15m}.
      - tip: Pour it into a shallow dish once cool, so it’s ready for dipping.
  - title: Cook the egg yolks
    text:
      - While the coffee cools, separate {4} **eggs**, putting the yolks in a heatproof bowl and the whites in a large, clean bowl.
      - Set the bowl of yolks over a pan of barely simmering water, not touching it, add {100 g} **sugar** and whisk constantly for {timer:8-10m}, until pale, thick enough to leave a ribbon trail, and about 70 °C on an instant-read thermometer.
      - tip: Cooking the yolks this way pasteurises them against salmonella and gives a thicker, more stable cream than whisking them raw and cold.
      - Remove from the heat and let cool to room temperature, whisking occasionally so the edges don’t set.
  - title: Make the cream
    text:
      - Beat {500 g} **mascarpone** briefly to loosen it, then fold it into the cooled yolk mixture in two additions until smooth.
      - tip: Folding mascarpone into a still-warm base can split it; make sure the yolk mixture has cooled to room temperature first.
      - Add a pinch of **salt** to the egg whites and whisk to stiff peaks.
      - Fold the whites into the mascarpone cream in three additions, cutting and turning gently to keep the air in, until no streaks remain.
      - tip: This one aerated cream is what holds tiramisu together. Making two separate creams, one with the yolks and one with the whites, and layering them apart (as some recipes do) gives two thinner, less stable layers instead of one.
  - title: Assemble
    text:
      - Dip {150 g} **Savoiardi biscuits**, briefly, 1–2 seconds a side, in the cooled coffee, just enough to soak through without turning soggy, and arrange them in a single, snug layer in the dish, trimming to fit.
      - Spread half of the cream over the biscuits and smooth it to the edges.
      - Dust lightly with {15 g} **cocoa powder** through a fine sieve.
      - Repeat with a second layer of dipped biscuits ({150 g}), the remaining cream, and a final dusting of {15 g} **cocoa powder**.
      - Grate {30 g} **dark chocolate** over the top.
  - title: Chill and serve
    text:
      - Cover and refrigerate for at least {wait:6h}, preferably overnight, for the cream to set and the flavours to meld.
      - tip: The yolks are cooked, but the whites stay raw. If that’s a concern for pregnant, elderly, very young or immunocompromised guests, use pasteurised egg whites instead, or leave this dessert out for them.
      - Slice and serve chilled, dusted with a little **icing sugar** if you like.

## Chef’s Notes

- **Make ahead:** Tiramisu is meant to be made ahead: it needs the chilling time to set, and it slices better, and tastes better, the next day. It keeps covered in the fridge for up to 2 days.
- **To serve:** A small glass of Vin Santo or a strong espresso alongside is the classic pairing.

## Allergens

- **Egg:** cooked yolks and raw whites in the cream.
- **Milk:** mascarpone; some dark chocolate also contains milk, so check the label.
- **Gluten:** Savoiardi biscuits. Substitute gluten-free ladyfingers if needed.
